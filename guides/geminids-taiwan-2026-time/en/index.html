2026 Geminids: planning around the peak in Taiwan

Taipei’s astronomy forecast places the 2026 Geminid peak near 10 p.m. on December 14, with a crescent Moon and favorable viewing conditions—not a guaranteed meteor count.

1. Mark this year’s predicted peak

Taipei’s December forecast gives December 14, around 22:00 Taiwan time, for the predicted Geminid maximum. Use the current local forecast.

2. Check the Moon conditions

The same forecast says the Moon is near a crescent that day, so viewing conditions are good. This Moon phase applies to 2026 and cannot be carried over to next year.

3. Read the chart’s reference time

The chart is set for December 14 at 20:30, facing east-northeast. That is the chart’s reference view, not the only place meteors can appear.

4. Look across the sky

NASA notes that Gemini contains the radiant, while meteors can appear throughout the sky. Keep a broad view.

5. Treat forecasts as forecasts

NASA recommends getting away from city lights and allowing time for dark adaptation. A predicted maximum helps scheduling, but viewing still needs suitable skies.
