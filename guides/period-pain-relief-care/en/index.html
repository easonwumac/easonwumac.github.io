Period pain: heat, gentle movement, and when to get help

Warmth, a warm bath, and gentle activity may ease cramps. Seek urgent care for severe or worsening pain that pain medicine has not relieved; see a doctor if pain stops you doing your usual daily activities.

1. Recognize the usual timing

Cramps often begin just before or at the start of a period, may spread to the back and thighs, and usually last up to three days.

2. Try warmth

NHS suggests a warm bath or shower, or a hot water bottle wrapped in a towel on the abdomen.

3. Try gentle movement or massage

Try gentle activity such as walking or yoga, or massage the abdomen and back; NHS also lists painkillers such as paracetamol or ibuprofen. These are comfort measures, not a diagnosis.

4. Get help for severe or worsening pain

NHS calls for urgent assessment when pelvic or period pain is severe or worse than usual and pain medicine has not helped.

5. Book care for recurring disruption

See a doctor if periods become more painful, heavier, or irregular, if pain disrupts daily activities, or if you bleed between periods.
