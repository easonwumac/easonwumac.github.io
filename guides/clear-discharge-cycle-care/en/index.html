Clear discharge during your cycle: what to watch for

Clear or white discharge without a strong odor is often normal and may be wetter near ovulation. Changes in color or smell, itching, or pain need medical advice.

1. Know what can be normal

Normal discharge can be clear or white, thick and sticky, or slippery and wet, without a strong or unpleasant odor.

2. Recognize cycle-related changes

Around ovulation, discharge may be clearer, wetter, thinner, and stretchy like raw egg white.

3. Do not self-diagnose by appearance

Changes in color, odor, or texture might be a sign of infection. NHS advises against self-diagnosis and says to see a GP if you are worried; appearance alone does not identify an infection.

4. Seek advice for symptoms or bleeding

Seek medical advice for itching, soreness, painful urination, pelvic pain, bleeding between periods or after sex, or more discharge than usual.

5. Wash externally and avoid douching

Gently wash the external skin with warm water and mild, unscented soap. Avoid scented products, fragranced wipes, and washing inside the vagina.
