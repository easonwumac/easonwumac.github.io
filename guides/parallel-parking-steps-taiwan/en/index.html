Parallel parking steps, with Taiwan's curb-distance rule

For parallel parking, find a legal space, signal, check blind spots, and back in slowly before straightening. Use official driving guidance for the maneuver and Taiwan's rules for the final parking position.

1. Check that parking is allowed

Taiwan prohibits parking where signs or markings forbid it or where it obstructs traffic. Check the posted rules and marked space rather than assuming any gap between cars is available.

2. Choose a space and pull alongside

The California DMV sequence starts with finding sufficient space and signaling, then pulling beside the vehicle ahead until your rear bumper aligns with the front of the space. Treat this as maneuver guidance alongside local parking rules.

3. Check mirrors and blind spots

The DMV calls for checking the rearview mirror and looking over your shoulder for traffic and pedestrians. Taiwan also requires reverse lights or a hand signal and careful, slow backing while watching others.

4. Back in and then steer away from the curb

The DMV describes backing into the space at roughly 45 degrees, then steering away from the curb as the rear wheel approaches it to straighten the car. Adjust forward and backward as needed while continuing to watch for others.

5. Apply Taiwan's 40-centimeter rule

Taiwan requires cars to park in the direction of traffic, with the outer edges of the front and rear tires no more than 40 centimeters from the curb or road edge. Follow any special local rules for time, position, or method.

6. Check before opening the door

After parking, check behind and beside you for pedestrians, bicycles, motorcycles, and other vehicles before opening the door, and exit only when it is safe.
