What to do about upstairs noise: records, building management, and police

Document when the upstairs noise occurs and what it sounds like, then contact the appropriate authority. Building management can address late-night neighbor disturbances; police handle noncontinuous or difficult-to-measure disturbances.

1. Describe the disturbance first

Record dates, times, locations, and what happened, with photos or video that explain the incident. These details tell the receiving authority where and when to investigate.

2. Distinguish household noise from regulated sources

The Ministry of Environment directs noncontinuous or difficult-to-measure disturbances to police. Construction and other regulated sources can be reported to environmental authorities; the floor the noise comes from does not determine jurisdiction.

3. Involve building management

If your building has a management committee, you can first give it the incident times and a specific description and ask it to act under the building's rules. When the noise is happening now or the committee cannot resolve it, the Ministry of Environment says to call 110 to reach the police.

4. Explain the situation when contacting police

For these neighbor disturbances in buildings without a management committee, government guidance lists 110. Explain the location and whether the disturbance is happening now; police handle the specified noncontinuous or difficult-to-measure sounds.

5. Use phone records to explain the timeline

Hearscape's store description includes monitoring while the screen is locked, threshold-triggered clips, and report export. It also labels measurements as reference-only; present them as a timeline, not an official violation finding.

Record only the noise you can hear in your own home, never other people’s private conversations or activities. Under Article 315-1 of Taiwan’s Criminal Code, secretly recording others’ non-public activities, speech or conversations without reason can lead to up to three years in prison, detention or a fine of up to NT$300,000.
