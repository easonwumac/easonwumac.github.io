Potted hydrangea care: light, watering, and pruning

Potted hydrangeas need attention to water and suitable shade under strong summer sun; the potting mix must both hold water and drain. When pruning after flowering, mind when flower buds form and do not keep cutting into autumn.

1. Choose suitable light

The agriculture guide lists partial sun and shade for hydrangeas. In intense summer heat and light, shading helps limit excessive water loss.

2. Watch moisture closely

Large leaves lose water quickly, and severe water shortage can kill the plant. Consider summer heat and strong light when checking moisture.

3. Balance retention and drainage

Official guidance recommends a mixed potting soil or sandy loam that drains well yet holds some water; do not focus on water retention alone and ignore the growing medium.

4. Do not prune too late

The ministry’s plant guide places natural flowering around May–June in Taiwan, with bud differentiation beginning after August; the final pruning should be no later than August.

5. Distinguish natural and forced flowering

Commercial growers can adjust flowering for Lunar New Year. The month you receive a blooming pot may differ from its natural flowering season.
