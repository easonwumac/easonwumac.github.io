Photographing the Milky Way with iPhone Night mode

Start with a dark sky and a stable phone, then use Night mode on a supported iPhone. Adjust the available capture time instead of assuming one setting works everywhere.

1. Start with a dark sky

NASA explains that from dark places far from bright city lights, the Milky Way looks like a faint band of light across the night sky. Check the sky conditions before you shoot.

2. Stabilize the phone

Use a secure surface or tripod and keep iPhone still until capture finishes. NASA also notes that some phones can photograph the night sky when stabilized.

3. Check that Night mode is active

On supported models, Night mode activates in low light and its icon turns yellow. Turning on flash or Live Photos disables it.

4. Adjust the available capture time

The number beside the Night mode icon is the capture time. Its slider can select a longer available exposure, then displays a countdown during capture.

5. Wait for capture to finish

A Night mode exposure may take several seconds. Hold still until it ends, then compare results with other available capture times.

Nightvane displays the Milky Way and its core and lets you change the viewing time to plan a direction; take the photo in iPhone’s Camera app.
