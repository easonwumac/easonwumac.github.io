How to build a water-rocket car: a Splash Car beginner’s guide

In Splash Car, your water-rocket car always launches at full power; whether it finishes the track depends on your build. Where a part sits changes what it does.

1. Drag parts to different spots

Parts go on the nose, roof, tail, sides, belly or tow hitch, and the same part works differently in each spot: a wing on the tail presses the car down for steadier corners, while on the roof it lifts the car to fly farther.

2. Press GO and see if it stops past the finish

When the build is ready, press GO and watch whether the car crosses the finish flag and then stops.

3. Learn from the failure screen

The failure screen says what went wrong, such as flying off a bend too fast, lacking speed for a loop, or landing nose-high and flipping, and it lights up the spots worth changing.

After two failed tries, a hint bulb walks you step by step to a three-star car. After a clear, “What you learned” explains the idea behind it in one sentence: centre of mass, lift, grip, drag, thrust and weight.

4. Know the parts

  • Wings, ballast, guide wheels, drag chutes, balloons, magnets, stabiliser fins, springs and spare water bottles
  • Nozzles, tyres and the amount of water can be changed too

5. Levels and stars

There are 50 toy-track levels in 9 chapters, with loops, ramps, ice, conveyor belts, tailwinds and headwinds, low bars and towers to knock down. Each level’s three stars float in the air, and purple secret stars unlock a secret level once you collect 5.

Splash Car needs no sign-up and plays offline; only the leaderboards use Game Center. It supports iPhone and iPad.

Free on the App Store
