Are iPhone decibel meters accurate? What to check about calibration before you report noise

Accuracy depends on the app and microphone's validation conditions. Results for one tested app do not validate every app, and entering a calibration offset does not make a phone an approved instrument.

1. Identify exactly what was validated

NIOSH's published accuracy and standards statements refer to its own sound level meter app. Its Type 2 statement requires a calibrated external microphone; it does not validate unrelated apps.

2. Check the microphone setup

Distinguish an internal phone microphone from an external one when reading accuracy claims. NIOSH explicitly includes a calibrated external microphone in its standards statement; iPhone compatibility alone is not equivalent evidence.

3. Read the calibration guidance before changing values

NIOSH supports calibration of internal and external microphones. For another app, follow that product's documentation rather than importing calibration procedures or error ranges from a different product.

4. Apply Taiwan's official measurement requirements

Taiwan's Noise Control Standards specify instruments, measurement locations, timing, and background-noise handling. A phone result alone does not demonstrate that those requirements were met.

5. Read the app's own limitations

Hearscape allows a custom calibration offset, but its store description also says it uses the phone microphone, is for reference, and is not a certified meter. Keep that limitation with any shared results.
