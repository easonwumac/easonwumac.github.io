Late period: when to test and when to see a doctor

Pregnancy, stress, and other changes can delay a period. Timing alone cannot establish the cause. Test if pregnancy is possible, and seek care if the delay persists.

1. Do not rely on the predicted date alone

NHS lists variable cycles, stress, sudden weight loss, being overweight, excessive exercise, breastfeeding, and some hormonal contraceptives among reasons for late or missed periods.

2. Test when pregnancy is possible

Most tests can be used from the first day of a missed period. If you do not know when it is due, test at least 21 days after your last unprotected sex.

3. Follow up a negative result

Testing too early can give an unreliable negative result. Retest after a few days if pregnancy is still possible; see a doctor after a second negative if your period has not arrived.

4. Know when to book an appointment

NHS advises medical review after three missed periods in a row, newly irregular periods, or missed periods with symptoms such as weight changes or tiredness.

5. Let the cause guide treatment

Treatment depends on the cause. A clinician may review your health and arrange tests; a delayed date on a calendar does not identify a condition.
