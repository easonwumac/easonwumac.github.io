Watering succulents: distinguish growth from dormancy

Identify the succulent and its dormant season before changing watering. Not every species rests in winter; dormancy generally calls for less water and a well-aerated mix.

1. Identify the plant

Succulents store water in roots, stems, or leaves and span different plant families. The label alone does not establish a watering routine.

2. Check the dormant season

The Seed Improvement and Propagation Station notes that succulents include winter-dormant and summer-dormant types, and some without obvious dormancy. Confirm the plant's growth habit first, then decide how to change watering through the seasons.

3. Reduce water during dormancy

Reduce water and provide appropriate shade during dormancy. Summer alone is not a reason to increase water for a dormant succulent.

4. Meet active-growth needs

Active growth needs adequate water and light. A reduced dormant-season supply should not automatically carry over into active growth.

5. Check the potting mix

Succulents generally need less water than other plants and a mix with good drainage and aeration. Review these conditions alongside watering.
