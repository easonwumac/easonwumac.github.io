How canal locks work: raising and lowering boats between water levels

A navigation lock raises or lowers a boat by changing the chamber's water level. In the gravity-operated system illustrated by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, valves control the water before the matching gates open.

1. Think of the chamber as a water elevator

The boat enters a chamber whose water level can change, then rises or falls with the surface. The Corps' diagram shows upper and lower water levels, gates at both ends, and filling and emptying valves.

2. Match the entry-side water level

For a downstream trip, close both sets of gates and the emptying valve, then open the filling valve until the chamber reaches the upper level. Open the upper gates for the boat to enter.

3. Close the gates before lowering the boat

With the boat inside, close both sets of gates and the filling valve, then open the emptying valve. Water flows toward the lower level and the boat descends with it.

4. Open the exit gates at the matching level

Once the chamber reaches the lower level, open the lower gates and let the boat leave. Reverse the operation to raise a boat. This system lets water seek its own level without pumps.

5. Read the game's separate gate rules

In Lockwater, tapping a gate permanently equalizes the water on both sides, and tapping a boat sends it through open gates toward its matching flag. Those puzzle rules differ from the real lock sequence above.

Lockwater has 120 canal levels. Gate order changes water levels, and boats run aground when the water is too shallow.
