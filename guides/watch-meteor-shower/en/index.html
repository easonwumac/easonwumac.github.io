How to watch a meteor shower: no telescope, just the right place and time

Watching a meteor shower takes no equipment; what matters most is a dark sky and patience.

1. Skip the telescope and binoculars

According to NASA, you do not need a telescope or binoculars; meteors can appear anywhere in the sky, so your eyes on the whole sky work best.

2. Get away from lights and bright moonlight

City lights and a bright Moon wash out fainter meteors, so check the Moon phase and moonrise before you go.

3. Lie back and give your eyes time

Find somewhere to lie back and watch for at least half an hour; use red light or dim your phone instead of looking at a bright screen.
