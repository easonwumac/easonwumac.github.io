How to estimate ovulation—and why it is not a safe-day guarantee

Ovulation often occurs about 10–16 days before the next period, rather than always on day 14. Calendar estimates cannot confirm ovulation or guarantee safe days.

1. Work backward from the next period

NHS describes roughly 10–16 days from ovulation to the next period. This is a general range, not a fixed countdown for everyone.

2. Do not assume day 14

Day 14 is associated with a regular 28-day cycle. It does not apply automatically to shorter or longer cycles.

3. Recognize discharge changes

Discharge can become thinner and stretchy, like raw egg white, near ovulation. NHS also notes that exact ovulation timing is difficult to pinpoint.

4. Do not assume the days after a period are safe

Pregnancy is possible soon after a period if ovulation occurs early or cycles are short. Unmarked calendar dates do not guarantee no pregnancy risk.

5. Ask about concerning changes

NHS advises seeing a doctor if you are concerned about changes in discharge. An app’s estimated ovulation date does not explain symptoms.
