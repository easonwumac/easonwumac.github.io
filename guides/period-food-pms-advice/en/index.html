What to eat for period discomfort: diet advice versus pain relief

Healthy eating and reducing caffeine, salt, and sugar may help some premenstrual symptoms. That is different from treating active cramps or promising immediate relief from food.

1. Separate PMS from active cramps

The Office on Women’s Health describes PMS symptoms before a period, including bloating, headaches, mood changes, and appetite changes.

2. Use diet changes for PMS support

Its advice is to choose healthy foods most of the time and avoid foods and drinks with caffeine, salt, and sugar in the two weeks before a period, which may lessen many PMS symptoms.

3. Do not treat a craving as a remedy

Food cravings can themselves be a PMS symptom. The agency’s diet guidance is not a promise that eating chocolate relieves cramps.

4. Use appropriate measures for cramps

NHS comfort measures for cramps include warmth, a wrapped hot water bottle, gentle activity, and massage—separate from dietary changes for PMS.

5. Seek care when symptoms disrupt life

Talk with a clinician if PMS affects daily life. For severe or worsening period pain unrelieved by pain medicine, NHS advises urgent assessment.
